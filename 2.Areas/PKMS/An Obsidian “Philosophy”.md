Personal knowledge management tools like Obsidian have limitless possibilities for organizing information. Having a philosophy aligns these knowledge tools to your needs, rather than using them as a means to escape from “real work”.

In this article, I will share with you my “obsidian philosophy” gained from trial and error of what worked for me in my 2 years of using obsidian. Remember that every personal knowledge system is personal to its creator. In my case, I use Obsidian as a knowledge organization system for research and graduate studies, as well as a “smart journal” where I connect thoughts and unique ideas.

## Philosophy

- Obsidian as a tool should be used only if it provides immediate value compared to other methods of note taking
- Obsidian is first and foremost a *note taking* tool, and for collecting and synthesizing literature notes
- Obsidian is only useful insofar as it is used wherever typing is the primary method of thinking
- If a tool exists that can more efficiently achieve a task with better pros vs. cons, that tool should be used
- Obsidian is useful only insofar as it is a better means of thinking than pen and paper.
- A limitation of Obsidian will always be [distraction](https://medium.com/@devinat11/distraction) when compared to analog methods such as pen and paper. Therefore, pen and paper should be used whenever deeper thinking is needed, and it does not become cumbersome to do so.
- During the semester or in times outside break, only *minor* changes should be made to the obsidian system, with the rest placed into [Obsidian Enhancement Ideas](https://medium.com/@devinat11/Obsidian%20Enhancement%20Ideas) for further processing when more time is allotted for the task.
- Obsidian itself can be an [escape](https://medium.com/@devinat11/escape) and [procrastination](https://medium.com/@devinat11/procrastination) if not used carefully.
- The primary usage of my Obsidian vault is the *application* of the Zettelkasten note taking system. If a document does not take advantage of the Zettelkasten system in any way, it should not be within my vaults.
- Obsidian should not be discussed via buzzwords, and should not be used as a [superioty complex](https://medium.com/@devinat11/superioty%20complex) mechanism
- i.e. no “I use obsidian, btw” comments, but a *recommendation* keeping in mind pros and cons

## Learning

- Wherever it is practical, the [principle of directness](https://medium.com/@devinat11/principle%20of%20directness) via means such as taking a practice exam or directly doing a task should be favored over more passive forms of learning, such as reading or taking notes
- Therefore, Obsidian should serve as a *supplement* for direct practice, and not a replacement

## Church and State

- Personal and Work/Study vaults will forever be separate
- This is because work life and personal life should not be mixed
- You can write about work in the personal vault since a lot of work has personal connotations
- An exception here is Readwise literature notes, since there is no way to split the syncing functionality between vaults

## Pros and Cons

## Pros

- Excellent for the Zettelkasten system of notetaking
- Can be used entirely offline, since its not a SAAS product
- Notetaking system endlessly extensible according to ones needs
- Promotes a flow of thinking
- Excellent typing experience
- Easy to connect notes together and see their relationships
- Facilitates a deeper understanding of notes to the bigger picture of a concept
- Facilitates a bottom up approach of thinking, wherein one thinks of multiple separate ideas and then combines them idea into a whole piece rather than top down wherein one starts with a bigger idea.
- Is private by default, with files only local to the computer (and synced via obsidian sync, which end to end encrypts the files as they are sent across the wire)
- Is fast
- Files are stored locally on the computer, so even if Obsidian the company goes away, the files are there to stay in standard
- Not opinionated
- Markdown format

## Cons

- Too many ways to do one thing
- Needs time to maintain and fix bugs and issues with workflow
- Not opinionated
- Very high barrier to entry
- Quite a few annoyances which can be fixed, but take a long time to fix
- Plugins are inherently privacy violations, since they involve execution of untrusted code
- But, they are necessary for the usefulness of Obsidian
- PDFs and images could be supported a bit better
- Not suitable for layman users, since programming knowledge is needed to use a lot of the important plugins
- Issues with syncing between devices
- Fixed through a combination of syncing software, but still a hassle

## Canvas, Kanban, and Slides

- A canvas, just like a kanban board or advanced slides/quarto is fundamentally a *different file type*, and does not represent a *singular atomic idea*. Therefore, it cannot be called a *Zettel* in the traditional sense.
- So, for those types of files, I have placed them into their own respective root directories, though if I wanted to, I could link to those files.
- The consequence of this is redundant directories, such as CS590 for both Zettelkasten and the Canvas to better structure these types of files.
- The point is that these files don’t have meaning as applied to the MOC and Zettel. A canvas is more of a *fleeting note* that can be used to *dump ideas*, and thereby can serve as an alternative to google keep but with first class support by the obsidian core team and all the linking goodies thereby involved.

## Publish

- MOCs should not be published, and Google Docs with the relevant notes should exist instead, with there being one large Zettel representing many notes as it answers some question instead.
- This allows a viewer not just a part of the obsidian ecosystem to access your notes in a way they understand.

## Fleeting Notes

- Google keep is used to write fleeting notes
- Alternatively, a canvas mindmap could be used
- Periodic notes within the personal vault are used as well for fleeting notes, if it happens that the note is directly relevant to the broader Zettelkasten system

## Privacy

- Any plugin should be checked for matters of stability and privacy prior to being installed.
- Any plugin which is connected to the cloud or an LLM in some way should be used sparingly only for the notes which the user does not believe leaks sensitive information.
- ex) The LangugeTool plugin, which checks for grammar on a given document, thereby first processing that document on the cloud
- ex) Any plugin which uses the GPT API, such as Smart Connections

## Folders vs. Links vs. Tags

- Folders are for separating things, and tags are for unifying things.
- Tags are specific to the *note philosophy*, and consists of the following:
- idea
- Atomic note to be fleshed out within Zettelkasten system
- MOC
- A means to provide an index for a collection of notes which have some mutual relationship
- literature
- A note meant to represent a piece of literature, parsed through means such as Readwise or Zotero
- presentation
- For use with Obsidian Advanced Slides plugin
- idea tags may be upgraded to MOCs and vice versa
- A link can be used while writing about a topic by using the word the note is associated with. Aliases help immensely in associating synonyms with the same note, and preserving the original meaning of the file name.

## Presentations

- A presentation is not a Zettel note in the sense that it need not
- Yet it is not a file in the sense of an image or a PDF, since it is dynamically changing based on Zettel and literature notes, and may be interacted with directly

## Collaboration

- Obsidian is a PKM (Personal Knowledge Management) tool, and therefore is not meant for collaboration within Obsidian itself
- But, it should always be taken into account as to how to efficiently transform atomic notes into a central document, such as a presentation or research paper, which can easily be exported outside of obsidian.
- Efforts should be made to make content within the vault understandable to an outside audience (written in Layman’s terms where it is proper to do so).

## Excalidraw

- Each drawing must be embedded into a file for the for the drawing to have any meaning
- Note that the two sided drawing/note is not used, i.e. a drawing should not
- A drawing can reference another drawing, which provides further meaning to the note that drawing was embedded in.
- A different tool for drawing should be used if it becomes too bothersome to draw in Obsidian Excalidraw

## Todoist

- If a note is not sent to todoist, then it is either:
- Meant for longer term building upon (like this document) and should be bookmarked
- Can be kept as is, wherein the status remains so long as the document doesn’t come to mind again
- Not important and can be deleted

## Bookmarks

- Meant for longer term content that I build upon as I am writing about a myriad of topics

## Red, Green, Blue

- No status: The file has not been processed, or is otherwise not important
- Red: The file has been “touched” in the sense that it has been seen, but it needs further thought and to be built upon
- Yellow: The file has been processed in at least one pass, and the note is beginning to form to become useful. Further fact checking and modification is needed to guarantee green status
- Green: The file has been fully integrated into the Zettelkasten system and is otherwise fully fleshed out, fact checked, and formatted to be useful outside the PKM system
